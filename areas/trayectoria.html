Trayectoria profesional

El empleo de entrada es donde la IA golpea primero, y de él depende el argumento de valor de un título. Egresados y empleadores son la mejor fuente para saber qué competencias deben cambiar en los programas.

Qué vimos
  • Las prácticas siguen tres reglas: con crédito, con acompañamiento y pagadas. Hay fondos de hasta 5.000 dólares por estudiante, y con aprendizaje aplicado la probabilidad de ser contratado en seis meses sube un 75 % (William & Mary).
  • La contratación ya está mediada por IA: entrevistas asíncronas y entrevistadores avatar. Se estima que el 35 % de los empleos de entrada pedirán habilidades de IA (NACE).
  • Preparación para entrevistas en cuatro rondas: taller, práctica con IA, práctica con asesor y entrevista con profesores, para más de 300 estudiantes (Montclair State University).
  • Los egresados como sensor del mercado: encuestas a miles de egresados, grupos focales y entrevistas para definir competencias (Elon University, The Ohio State University, Juniata College).
Por qué le importa al área

El empleo de entrada es donde la IA golpea primero, y de él depende el argumento de valor de un título. Egresados y empleadores son la mejor fuente para saber qué competencias deben cambiar en los programas.

Recomendaciones

  1. Crear una serie de conversaciones periódicas con egresados de distintas cohortes sobre cómo la IA cambia su trabajo, y llevar los hallazgos a los programas.
  2. Preparar a los estudiantes para entrevistas mediadas por IA, combinando práctica con IA y asesoría humana.
  3. Agregar preguntas sobre el uso de IA al seguimiento de prácticas y de egresados.

Preparing Purpose-Ready Graduates for an AI-Augmented Workforce: A Cross-Functional Model for Curriculum, Pathways, and Career Readiness

Miércoles 28 de octubre · 4:00 PM
William & Mary
Descripción

This proposal shares William & Mary’s model to prepare students for an AI-accelerated labor market through linked curricular, cocurricular, and career-facing design. The session will present a practical framework organized around five connected domains: · Curricular redesign , including AI-related pathways, interdisciplinary course and program design, and stronger attention to AI fluency alongside ethics, communication, and human judgment. · Accelerated bachelor’s-to-master’s pathways (BMAPs) that shorten time to advanced preparation. · Experiential preparation , from research and capstone or prototype work to internship pipelines linking government, industry, research, and alumni networks. · Undergraduate research engagement , ensuring every undergraduate can participate in cutting-edge research . · Professional digital communication , including preparation for digital interviews and other AI-mediated screening environments through campus career-development partnerships. This framework is informed by William & Mary’s AI-related academic initiatives, BMAP structures, career resources like Big Interview, and a broader human-centered institutional conversation about responsible AI.

Creativity Unleashed: How a University-Wide AI Competition Builds Student Confidence, Cross-Disciplinary Engagement, and Workforce Readiness

Miércoles 28 de octubre · 5:25 PM
Kean University
Descripción

How can institutions encourage students from every discipline to engage meaningfully with artificial intelligence, even those who have no technical background? This session presents a model that answers that question through a university-wide AI competition designed to be radically inclusive: no coding required, no restrictions on medium, and a creative theme broad enough to attract students from architecture to business to the liberal arts. Participants use AI tools of their choosing to produce original creative works, submitting both the finished piece and the full conversation log that documents their process. This transparency requirement turns the competition into a learning artifact: students see that success depends not on technical sophistication but on the quality of their ideas, the originality of their vision, and the critical judgment they apply when directing AI output. The presenter will share the competition’s design, its integration into the broader cocurricular ecosystem, and early evidence of impact on student attitudes toward AI as a creative and professional resource.

Responding to Student AI Resistance through Career Readiness

Jueves 29 de octubre · 11:00 AM
Eckerd College
Descripción

Students, for a variety of reasons, express hesitation to use AI to help with the job search process, even as career educators and employers consider AI essential. According to the 2026 Job Outlook Spring Update from the National Association of Colleges and Employers, entry-level jobs requiring AI skills are up 25 percent from fall 2025 with nearly a third of entry-level jobs requiring AI skills. Nearly 66 percent of employers are discussing AI in relation to tasks or functions within specific jobs. For students entering a competitive job market, AI can help students rethink their own competencies and skills and then translate them into marketable skills and can accelerate the job search process. Herein is one avenue where AI resistance and critical AI literacy intersect. Focusing on AI in a specific context (the job market), using AI in guided, focused ways, and deliberately interrogating the outputs of AI so that they are usable in the job search process, enable productive conversations about both the benefits and limitations of AI. This interactive presentation brings together both the director of the Career Center and the assistant dean for AI from a small, liberal arts institution to share how they help students overcome AI resistance and develop critical AI literacy through a career-focused approach.

Bridging the Readiness Gap

Jueves 29 de octubre · 11:08 AM · 2 materiales (solicite acceso a SharePoint)
Adams State University
Descripción

As generative artificial intelligence reshapes the global labor market, the gap between college education and workforce readiness is growing. This Pecha Kucha presentation shares the findings on how Adams State is navigating this transition from pedagogical caution to professional necessity. This report shares how instructors are moving away from AI as a tool to cheat on tests and assignments and toward AI as a collaborator and thought partner. Most faculty agreed that AI skills are important for students entering the workforce, but less than half of the faculty scaffolded AI use into coursework. This mirrors national findings from the Cengage 2025 report, where 55 percent of graduates felt their programs failed to provide job-specific AI skills. AI literacy is required for today’s workforce as evinced by the "56% AI skills premium" and shifting industry demands. This gap analysis calls for structured support, upskilling, incentivization, and policy transparency to ensure the professoriate is human-centered and AI-proficient to prepare today’s students for work in the age of AI. ASU’s data confirms that faculty support for AI literacy is high (76 percent), but how to implement AI literacy remains fragmented due to a lack of institutional incentives and low uptake of the AI training offered multiple times each semester.

Building the Collaborative AI Competencies Students Actually Need

Jueves 29 de octubre · 12:00 PM
Appalachian State University · Voltage Control
Descripción

The hard part isn't the AI. It's getting teams to work differently together with it. While most of higher education's response to AI has centered on academic integrity and individual productivity, the workplace has moved on. Corporate environments now expect teams to visualize systems together, and develop shared judgment about when to "call in" AI as what Gartner terms a "toolmate." Students graduating without these skills are already behind. This theory-to-practice session shares lessons from an innovative doctoral-level online course built on collaborative AI workflows grounded in learning sciences research, and developed in partnership with Voltage Control, a facilitation-led AI transformation consultancy that works with organizations like Google, Autodesk, and Nike to build these team-based AI competencies. Rather than students working in isolation with individual ChatGPT or Claude accounts, the course integrates AI features within a shared visual collaboration platform, Miro, enabling students to articulate why collaborative AI practice, and not just individual productivity, is the competency gap employers are urgently trying to close. This will be accomplished by demonstrating how to visualize complex processes and workflows in real time while comparing outputs across different AI models within a unified workspace. This process allows learners to develop metacognitive awareness about when to leverage AI and when to protect human judgment.

Building a Flexible Micro-Credential Pathway Model for Student Innovation and Leadership Development

Jueves 29 de octubre · 2:15 PM
Iowa State University
Descripción

Iowa State University's College of Agricultural and Life Sciences (CALS) Pathways to Innovation and Leadership program is implementing a flexible, scalable micro-credential model that bridges academic learning with hands-on experience and career readiness, with measurable impact . Launched in spring 2025, the program integrates leadership, innovation, and professional skill development through structured workshops, mock interviews, individualized planning, and public presentations that lead to institutionally approved micro-credentials. In just the program’s second semester, more than 500 students across 27 majors have joined, and over 400 have participated in workshops that leverage AI to help students reflect and build confidence in articulating their skills. Join this interactive session to learn from our successes, challenges, and data-driven improvements in creating a program that students embrace.

Beyond Experimentation: Implementing an Adaptable Custom GPT Framework for Major and Career Exploration

Jueves 29 de octubre · 3:00 PM · 3 materiales (solicite acceso a SharePoint)
Montclair State University
Descripción

As artificial intelligence becomes a default resource for many students, institutions face a pressing question: how can we move from unstructured student AI use to intentional, mission-aligned support? As a case study, this session uses Major Mentor , Montclair State University's no-code Custom GPT built with ChatGPT, to present a practical framework for implementing institution-specific, student-facing AI. Major Mentor was designed to extend, not replace, human support by offering students a low-stakes, accessible space to reflect on interests, explore academic pathways, and connect to campus resources. Presenters will examine what higher education professionals can contribute to institution-wide AI strategy through a human-centered, equity-minded implementation model. Participants will discuss how institution-specific AI can support student engagement, belonging, and workforce readiness while remaining grounded in ethical practice. The session will also highlight early implementation insights, including student engagement patterns, workshop integration, and ongoing maintenance challenges such as content updates, guardrails, and transparency. Presenters will broach the opportunity for cross-functional collaboration by way of Red Hawk Recon , an iteration of Major Mentor , adapted for Montclair’s Veteran and Military Services. This Custom GPT serves as a personalized support guide designed specifically for student veterans, service members, and military-affiliated students.

From Uncertainty to Direction: Using AI-Supported Reflection to Guide Career Discovery

Jueves 29 de octubre · 3:00 PM · 2 materiales (solicite acceso a SharePoint)
University of Southern Indiana
Descripción

As artificial intelligence becomes more visible across higher education, institutions are exploring how AI can support advising, career development, and student reflection without replacing the human relationships at the center of student support. This session introduces a reflective career discovery framework that combines a structured self-exploration worksheet with AI-supported analysis to help students recognize patterns in their strengths, interests, and motivations. Rather than prescribing career outcomes, AI is used to organize student reflections and surface themes that guide more meaningful advising conversations. The framework is currently used across multiple areas of one university, including advising centers, career services, counseling services, and academic programs. Participants will see examples of the assessment worksheet, the prompts used to guide AI analysis, and how the results become the starting point for reflective conversations that help students move from uncertainty toward clearer academic and career direction. When students can connect their education to meaningful goals, engagement and persistence often improve.

AI4VA: An Agile Model for Providing Equitable Access to AI Workforce Readiness

Viernes 30 de octubre · 11:25 AM · 1 material (solicite acceso a SharePoint)
University of Virginia
Descripción

According to a recent Pearson study , only 14 percent of current graduates report high proficiency applying AI to professional workflows, while 53 percent of employers struggle to find AI-ready candidates. Closing that gap requires institutions to act quickly, and cocurricular models offer a critical advantage: they can move at the speed of the labor market without waiting for curriculum revision cycles. In this session we present UVA's AI4VA , a free, virtual cocurricular program that pairs student teams with Virginia small businesses and nonprofits to engage in real-world AI consulting projects. Students earn credentials through Grow with Google's AI Professional Certificate, then apply those skills via six-week virtual employer projects across five tracks—organizational strategy, data and analytics, operations and process automation, marketing and e-commerce, and AI literacy and enablement. The result is a two-pronged outcome: industry-recognized credentials and hands-on experience that aligns with students’ and employers’ needs. AI4VA is a replicable model for institutions seeking to rapidly provide equitable access to applied AI skill-building. AI4VA is designed for all students regardless of major, year, or AI experience level. Because it is virtual, self-paced, and free, it reaches students who cannot access traditional internships—student-athletes, caregivers, students working in jobs unrelated to their career goals, and those without transportation or financial flexibility.
